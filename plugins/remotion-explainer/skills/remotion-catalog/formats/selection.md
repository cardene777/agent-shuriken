# Selection

The catalog is not a shelf of finished templates.
For the question a scene answers, search the form, the composition, the typography, and the motion apart, among examples that are already built, and borrow only the mechanism of each.
Never take over an example's subject, copy, colors, or fixed coordinates.

## Before searching

Write one sentence with the viewer's question, the verb the picture has to show, whether there are subtitles, how much text the scene needs, and the structure to avoid.
Then search each layer with English words taken from that sentence, one search per layer.

| Layer | Search for | Choose |
|---|---|---|
| Form | What the main object looks like: a device, a document, a diagram, a person | One entry, or `bespoke` when none has the subject's own name and state |
| Composition | How the parts sit in space: a comparison, a sequence, a route, a stack | One entry whose arrangement itself answers the viewer's question |
| Typography | How the text is read: a title, a caption, a label | At most one entry, borrowing only the timing of the reading |
| Motion | How the state changes: a reveal, a route traced, a shape that changes | One entry, chosen only after the form and the composition are approved |

## Rules

- Choose a composition only when it is a visual answer to the viewer's question, never for how striking it looks
- Choose a form only when it has the subject's concrete name and state. When no entry fits, the form is `bespoke`; a generic round icon or card does not stand in for it
- Choose the motion after the still form and composition are approved, and never bend the form to fit a motion chosen first
- From a typography entry borrow only how the content is read; design the shown text, font, line breaks, anchor, and hold for the target video again
- The files of an entry are where its mechanism is built, not something to copy as a whole scene
- An entry may have fixed sizes, a system font, a dark gradient, fixtures, textures, fonts, or packages such as `@remotion/motion-blur`; adapt each to the target canvas and type before use
- Keep the entry's ID, source, commit, license, and files in the record of the scene. When code under Apache-2.0 is changed and shared, keep its license and state the changes; when code under MIT is copied, keep its copyright and license notice
- A good-looking entry does not skip the review of the subtitle zone, the settled frame, normal speed, and the subject's own look

## One scene, one layout

Do not merge several finished layouts into one scene.
The form gives the main object its look, the composition the structure of the space, the typography the timing of the text, and the motion the change of state.
When two chosen entries do the same job, drop one.
