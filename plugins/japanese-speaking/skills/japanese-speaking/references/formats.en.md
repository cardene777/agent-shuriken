# How to build each format

Before writing, choose one format, lay out who says what in each part, and then write.
The parts are a guide, and you skip a part that has no material.

## Reading out news and talking about it

One person reads a news item, and then the two talk about that news.

1. The reader reads straight through what happened, who released it, and the main numbers and results. Hearsay is closed with 「〜ということです」 ("it is said that ~") or 「〜としています」 ("~ states that").
2. The partner reacts. The partner says, in their own words, what surprised them and what caught their interest.
3. Talk about why it matters. The speaker who knows a term explains it within the flow of the talk.
4. Talk about what is not known yet, the conditions, and the limits.
5. Include one difference between the two speakers' views.
6. Close with who it helps.

The reading states all the facts, and the discussion parts add no new facts.
NPR builds one item of a two-way as "what happened", "background and why it matters", and "what is not known yet and what happens next".

## Small talk

A short talk at the start of a show or between items.

1. After the greeting and the date, say a cue that starts the small talk, such as 「ニュースの前に、」 ("before the news,").
2. Bring up one topic, and the partner responds in their own words.
3. Exchange two to four turns, responding with back-channel words and 「ね」「よ」.
4. End with a line that returns to the main topic.

## Interview

An interviewer and a speaker dig into one topic.

1. The interviewer introduces the topic and the speaker.
2. The interviewer asks on behalf of the listeners and responds by repeating words from the speaker's answers.
3. The speaker's answers vary between long and short, and the interviewer restates the main points.
4. The interviewer ends by briefly summing up what was learned.

## Solo talk

Narration, solo podcasts, and voiceover for videos.

1. Start with the conclusion, or with a question to the listener.
2. Move forward with one piece of information per sentence, and address the listener with 「〜んです」 and 「〜ですよね」.
3. At the breaks between parts, say a cue that changes the topic, such as 「で、」 ("so,") or 「そういえば」 ("by the way").
4. End by restating the main point in one sentence.

## Speech script

A script that a person reads aloud at a presentation, a greeting, or a briefing session.

1. First say what the talk is about and the conclusion.
2. In each part, show where you are with 「1つ目は」 ("the first is") and 「次に」 ("next").
3. Repeat numbers and proper nouns.
4. At the end, restate the conclusion.

## Round-table

Three or more people talk about one topic. One host runs the talk, and the other speakers give their views.

1. The host greets the listeners and introduces the topic and each speaker, one at a time, by name and role.
2. The host names a speaker and hands them a question. Listeners cannot tell who is speaking from the voice alone, so the host says the name at the start of the question.
3. In each beat, every speaker talks at least once. The host hands the turn to a speaker who has not talked yet, with 「〇〇さんはどうですか？」 ("what do you think, ~?").
4. The speakers may also pick up each other's words. Include one difference in views.
5. The host briefly sums up what each speaker said and ends with a closing line.
