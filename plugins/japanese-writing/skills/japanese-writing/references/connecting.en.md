# How to connect sentences

This reference was made by editing two documents of Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs: 「公用文作成の考え方」 (Approach to Writing Official Documents) and 「分かり合うための言語コミュニケーション（報告）」 (Language Communication for Mutual Understanding: Report).
The first is a 2022 proposal by the Council for Cultural Affairs, and the second is a 2018 report by the Council's Japanese Language Subdivision.
From the two documents, only the guidance useful for writing short text that tells people something was chosen and rewritten in this plugin's words.
The agency did not write this text, and the agency does not endorse this reference.
This English version is a translation of the plugin's edited Japanese text, made by the plugin, not by the agency.
The source URLs and terms of use are in the plugin's `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

Read this when writing or fixing text that tells people something.
Chat posts, handoffs, reports, and requests are such text.
Other skills that write chat or handoff text may also read it.

## 1. Keep each sentence short and join sentences with conjunctions

Split a long sentence at full stops (句点) into several sentences, and show the relation between the split sentences with conjunctions.
When a sentence goes over 50 to 60 characters, check whether it has become hard to read.
In chat posts, make it shorter still.
If you only split without placing a conjunction, the reader has to supply the relation between the sentences themselves, and the text looks chopped up.

Do not drop information the reader needs in order to make it short.
First narrow down what to tell, then join the remaining points with short sentences and conjunctions.

## 2. Conjunctions for each relation

| Relation | Example conjunctions | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Reason and result | そのため、だから、したがって、ついては ("for that reason", "so", "therefore", "accordingly") | When the previous sentence is the reason and the next sentence is its result, or a request that follows from it |
| Contrast | しかし、ところが、ただし ("however", "but then", "provided that") | When continuing with something different from what the reader expects from the previous sentence. 「ただし」 adds an exception or a condition to the previous sentence |
| Addition | なお、また、さらに、例えば ("note that", "also", "moreover", "for example") | When adding something else to the previous sentence. 「なお」 adds a note that is off the main line |
| Order | まず、次に、最後に ("first", "next", "finally") | When the reader acts by following steps |
| Rephrasing | つまり、要するに ("that is", "in short") | When restating the previous sentence |

Put a comma (読点) after a conjunction.
Do not place a conjunction where the relation is clear from the content of the sentences.
Placing a conjunction at the head of every sentence makes the text harder to read instead.
Do not use 「そして」 ("and then") several times in a row.

Words that take up the previous sentence also connect sentences, just like conjunctions.
Placing 「このうち」 ("of these"), 「これに対しては」 ("against this"), or 「その場合」 ("in that case") at the head of a split sentence keeps its link to the previous sentence.

## 3. Do not keep a sentence going with conjunctive particles or the suspended form

Continuing a sentence by stacking the conjunctive particles 「が」 ("but"), 「ので」 ("because"), and 「て」 ("and") puts many points into one sentence.
The suspended form (中止法) does the same: it puts the predicate in the continuative form (連用形) and continues without ending the sentence.
Both make it hard to tell what the writer wants to say.
End the sentence where it was continued, and place a conjunction that shows the relation between the two parts.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| 昨日、A社から当社には発注できないと連絡があったと報告しましたが、詳しく尋ねたところ最終的な決定ではないと分かったので、対策を練り直して受注に努めます。 ("Yesterday I reported that Company A told us they could not place an order with us, but when I asked for details I learned it was not a final decision, so we will rework our approach and try to win the order.") | 昨日、A社から当社には発注できないと連絡があったと報告しました。しかし、これは最終的な決定ではないとのことです。ついては、対策を練り直して受注に努めます。 ("Yesterday I reported that Company A told us they could not place an order with us. However, this is not a final decision. Accordingly, we will rework our approach and try to win the order.") |
| 来週の定例は会議室が取れなかったので、オンラインに切り替えますが、資料は前日までに共有フォルダへ置いてもらい、議題があれば木曜までに知らせてください。 ("We could not book a meeting room for next week's regular meeting, so we will switch to online, but please have the materials put in the shared folder by the day before, and if you have agenda items let us know by Thursday.") | 来週の定例は会議室が取れませんでした。そのため、オンラインに切り替えます。資料は前日までに共有フォルダへ置いてください。また、議題があれば木曜までに知らせてください。 ("We could not book a meeting room for next week's regular meeting. For that reason, we will switch to online. Please put the materials in the shared folder by the day before. Also, if you have agenda items, let us know by Thursday.") |

## 4. Keep one point per sentence

Deal with one point in one sentence, and end the sentence where the point changes.
Do not make several subject-predicate pairs inside one sentence.
At the head of each sentence after the split, place a conjunction from section 2 or a word that takes up the previous sentence.

## 5. Put the conclusion first, then the reasons and details

Give the conclusion in the first sentence or the first paragraph, then write the reasons, the evidence, and the details.
Avoid writing in a way where the reader cannot tell what you want to say until reaching the end.
In the sentences after the conclusion, place 「そのため」 ("for that reason"), 「なぜなら」 ("because"), or 「例えば」 ("for example") to show how they connect to the conclusion.
Leave concrete examples and fine details for later, and attach them separately when there are many.

## 6. Order things by the reader's steps

Write what the reader does in the order of the steps the reader follows, and show the order with 「まず」 ("first") and 「次に」 ("next").
Also writing what the reader does not need to do makes the reader hesitate less.
When listing three or more items, use a bulleted list.

## 7. Word order and subjects

Base a sentence on the order 「いつ」 ("when"), 「どこで」 ("where"), 「誰が」 ("who"), 「何を」 ("what"), 「どうした」 ("did what").
Write so the relation between subject and predicate can be read, and do not change the subject partway through a sentence.
Keep a modifier close to the word it modifies, and a demonstrative close to what it refers to.

## 8. Check after writing

1. Whether any sentence over 50 to 60 characters remains
2. Whether any sentence links three or more points with 「が」, 「ので」, 「て」, or the suspended form
3. Whether the relation between split sentences is shown with a conjunction or a word that takes up the previous sentence
4. Whether a conjunction is placed at the head of every sentence
5. Whether the conclusion is in the first sentence or the first paragraph
6. Whether information the reader needs was dropped in order to make it short
